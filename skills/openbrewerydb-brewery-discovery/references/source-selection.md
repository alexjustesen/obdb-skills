# Source selection

Evaluate every external inventory before comparison. Record the publisher, URL, jurisdiction/scope, publication or update date, retrieval date, licensing terms, and known omissions.

## Authority

Use this general order, while accounting for the fact being verified:

1. Brewery-operated website or official social profile for its current name, locations, public operation, and contact details.
2. Regulator or government license/open-data source for legal entity, licensed premises, address, and license status.
3. Brewery guild or trade association for industry membership and regional discovery.
4. Local government, tourism authority, or economic-development directory.
5. Reputable reporting for openings, closures, moves, ownership changes, and rebrands.
6. Commercial aggregators, maps, review sites, and user-maintained directories as leads only.

Authority is field-specific. A current brewery site can establish its customer-facing name while a regulator establishes the licensed premises. Guild membership is not proof that every member operates an eligible brewery location.

## Recency

- Record publication/update and retrieval dates separately.
- Prefer recent evidence for status, address, website, phone, and branding.
- A newer closure or relocation report can outweigh an older official directory entry.
- An undated page is weaker for volatile facts. Corroborate it with a dated source.
- Archived or stale inventories remain useful for historical names but should not establish current operation alone.

## Licensing and reuse

- Confirm an explicit open-data license, public-domain status, terms allowing reuse, or permission before copying/bulk-normalizing a list.
- Record attribution, share-alike, noncommercial, and redistribution requirements.
- Website visibility does not imply permission to scrape or republish its compiled list.
- If terms are absent, restrictive, or unclear, do not reproduce the list. Use individual entries only as research leads where permitted and independently verify facts with reusable/primary sources.
- Do not bypass authentication, robots controls, rate limits, access controls, or technical restrictions.
- The final report should cite sources and summarize facts; avoid embedding an entire third-party inventory unless its license permits that use.

## Selection test

A source is suitable for systematic comparison only when:

- Its geographic and establishment scope is understood.
- Its provenance and publisher are identifiable.
- Its update age is acceptable for the audit or explicitly caveated.
- Its licensing permits the intended extraction and comparison.
- Its fields can be normalized without inventing values.

Use multiple complementary sources where practical. Disagreement is a research signal, not permission to choose the most convenient value.
